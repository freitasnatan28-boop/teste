Cats love to run through tunnels, ambush each other from the far end, and nap in the middle where no one can see them. Fabric tunnels from the pet store collapse and get torn; a cardboard tunnel is sturdier, free, and you can make it as long or as twisty as your floor space allows.

## What you need

- 3 or 4 long, narrow boxes of similar size (boxes from shoes, boots, wrapping paper or long products work well)
- Or 2 or 3 large shipping boxes you can cut into strips
- Utility knife, metal ruler, pencil
- Paper tape or PVA glue
- Optional: one bigger box for a "rest stop" in the middle

Remove staples, plastic tape and labels from every box before you start.

## How wide should the tunnel be?

| Cat | Tunnel inside width and height |
|---|---|
| Kitten | 6 to 7 in (15 to 18 cm) |
| Average adult cat | 8 to 10 in (20 to 25 cm) |
| Large cat | 10 to 12 in (25 to 30 cm) |

Your cat should be able to walk through without crouching hard and turn around in the rest stop. A tunnel that is too tight will be ignored; one that is too wide stops feeling like a tunnel.

## Step 1: Open the boxes into sleeves

Cut both short ends off each long box so it becomes an open sleeve. If your boxes are too tall, cut them down and tape the open side closed, so each sleeve has a floor, two walls and a roof.

**No long boxes?** Cut large shipping boxes into panels, then fold each panel into a square tube along the flutes (folds are cleanest along the flute direction) and glue the overlapping edge. Score the fold lines first with a butter knife.

## Step 2: Join the sleeves

Push the end of one sleeve about 2 inches (5 cm) inside the next, like a telescope, and glue or tape the overlap from the outside. If the sleeves are the same size, cut a short collar from a scrap strip, wrap it around the joint, and glue it.

Keep tape on the **outside** only, where your cat won't chew it.

## Step 3: Add a rest stop (optional)

Place a larger box in the middle of the run. Cut a hole on each side that matches your tunnel opening and slot the sleeves into it. This gives your cat a little room to turn around, nap, or wait in ambush.

## Step 4: Cut peek holes

Add a few round holes along the top and sides, about 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) across. Peek holes:

- let light in, so the tunnel is less intimidating;
- give a wand toy something to poke through;
- let cats watch each other, which makes chasing games more fun.

Round off every cut with a coin or bottle cap as a template. Rounded holes are stronger and do not snag fur.

## Step 5: Shape the run

A straight tunnel is fine, but an L or a zigzag is more exciting, because your cat cannot see what's around the corner. Use the rest stop box as the corner piece.

Put the tunnel against a wall or a sofa so it does not slide around when your cat charges through. A strip of rug grip underneath helps on smooth floors.

## Games to play

- **Wand toy at the exit.** Wiggle a wand toy just past the end of the tunnel, then whisk it away as your cat appears.
- **Peek-a-boo.** Poke the end of a wand toy through a peek hole.
- **Treat trail.** Drop treats along the tunnel for shy cats.
- **Two-cat chase.** In multi-cat homes, tunnels give cats a way to play chase without direct confrontation.

Put wand toys away when the game ends. String on wand toys is dangerous if swallowed.

## Safety and upkeep

- Check joints weekly and re-glue anything that is coming apart.
- Recycle the tunnel when it gets crushed, damp or chewed.
- If your cat starts eating the cardboard, switch to a fabric tunnel and read [is cardboard safe for cats?](/is-cardboard-safe-for-cats/)

## Combine it with other projects

A tunnel connects nicely to a [one-box cat house](/diy-cardboard-cat-house/) or to the base of a [cardboard cat castle](/cardboard-cat-castle/), giving your cat a whole cardboard "territory" to patrol.
