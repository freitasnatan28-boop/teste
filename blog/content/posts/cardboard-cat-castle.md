A cardboard cat castle is the weekend version of the [one-box cat house](/diy-cardboard-cat-house/). You stack and connect several boxes into towers and tunnels, which gives your cat somewhere to climb, hide and look down on the household from above.

The most common problem with cardboard castles is not that cats ignore them. It is that they wobble, lean, and eventually tip over the first time a cat leaps onto the top. This plan focuses on stability first.

## Why height matters to cats

Cats feel safer when they can watch their surroundings from a raised spot. Vertical space also gives cats in multi-cat homes a way to avoid each other without conflict. The [AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1098612X13477537) recommend that each cat have access to resting places, including elevated ones.

A castle that is two or three boxes tall is plenty for a cardboard build. Anything higher should be anchored to a wall.

## What you need

- 3 to 5 sturdy boxes in different sizes. The largest one becomes the base.
- 1 extra box for cutting tunnels, ramps and roof pieces
- Utility knife, metal ruler, pencil
- PVA glue and paper tape (or a hot glue gun, used carefully)
- Something heavy for the base: a few books, a bag of rice sealed in a zip bag, or a ceramic tile
- Optional: an old towel for each level

Double-wall boxes are worth looking for here. Boxes from appliance, liquor or grocery stores are often stronger than standard shipping boxes. See [the best cardboard for pet projects](/best-cardboard-for-pet-projects/).

## Plan the layout first

Sketch your castle before cutting anything. A reliable layout is:

1. **A wide, low base box** on the floor, ideally the largest box you have.
2. **A smaller box on top**, set toward the back so the front of the base becomes a ledge.
3. **A top tower** that is the smallest and lightest box.

Each box should be no taller than it is wide. Tall, narrow boxes are the ones that tip.

## Step 1: Reinforce the base

Weight is your friend. Place your heavy item inside the base box at the back, and glue a second layer of cardboard over it as a false floor so your cat cannot reach it. Then tape the base box closed along its bottom seams.

## Step 2: Cut the doors and the connecting holes

Cut an entrance door 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm) wide in the front of the base, with a 2-inch (5 cm) lip at the bottom.

Next, place the second box on the base where it will sit and trace around it. Inside that outline, cut a **connecting hole** at least 7 inches (18 cm) wide through the roof of the base. Cut a matching hole in the floor of the upper box. Your cat climbs from one level to the next through these holes.

Keep at least 2 inches (5 cm) of solid cardboard around every hole so the boxes keep their strength.

## Step 3: Stack and glue

Spread glue around the outline on top of the base, set the second box in place, and press it down. Put a few books on top while it dries. Repeat with the top tower.

Then tape every seam where two boxes meet, inside and out. Glue holds the boxes together; tape keeps them from peeling apart when a cat jumps.

## Step 4: Add windows, lookouts and a tunnel

- **Windows**: a few square or arched openings 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) wide on each level.
- **Lookout**: leave the top of the tower open, or cut its flaps into battlements and fold the rest flat to make a platform.
- **Tunnel**: tape a long, narrow box to the side of the base, open at both ends, to make a crawl-through.
- **Ramp** (for kittens and older cats): fold a strip of double cardboard into a gentle slope from the floor to the first ledge and glue a scratcher-style strip of corrugated cardboard on top for grip.

## Step 5: The tip test

Before your cat gets near it, push the top of the castle firmly from the front and the sides. It should not rock. If it does:

- add more weight to the base,
- move the upper boxes further back over the base,
- or place the castle against a wall and tape the back to a heavy piece of furniture.

A castle that wobbles even once can scare a cat off it permanently.

## Step 6: Decorate safely

Draw bricks with a marker on the outside, paint the outside with water-based non-toxic paint and let it dry for several days, or leave it plain brown. Do not decorate the inside, and skip glitter, ribbons, pompoms, string and anything small that could be pulled off and swallowed. Our [safe glue, paint and tape guide](/safe-glue-paint-and-tape-for-pet-diy/) explains why.

## Introducing the castle

Put a treat or a pinch of catnip on each level, and drop a familiar blanket into the base. Then walk away. Most cats explore on their own terms, often at night.

## Maintenance

Inspect the castle every week. Retape loose seams, and replace any box that is crushed, soggy or being chewed. Cardboard is meant to be temporary, and the good news is that the next delivery brings new building material.
