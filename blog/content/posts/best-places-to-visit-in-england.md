England is small enough that most of this list is within three hours of London by train. That makes it easy to combine a few days in the capital with historic cities and countryside. It is also a country where the weather can change four times in an afternoon, so pack a rain jacket whatever the season.

## 1. London

London could fill a month. Many of its great museums are free, including the British Museum, the National Gallery, the Natural History Museum and Tate Modern. Walk along the South Bank of the Thames from the London Eye to Tower Bridge, visit the Tower of London and Westminster Abbey, and spend an evening at a West End show.

**Tip** You can pay for the Underground, buses and most trains in London with a contactless card or phone. Daily fares are capped automatically.

## 2. Bath

Bath is named after the Roman baths at its centre, built around England's only natural hot springs. The whole city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with honey-coloured Georgian terraces such as the Royal Crescent. After the Roman Baths museum, you can bathe in the thermal water yourself at the modern Thermae Bath Spa.

**Tip** Bath is about 90 minutes from London by train, but it deserves a night.

## 3. The Cotswolds

Rolling hills, dry-stone walls and villages built of golden limestone. Bibury, Castle Combe, Bourton-on-the-Water and Chipping Campden are the famous ones, and the best way to see them is on the footpaths between them.

**Tip** Public transport between villages is limited. A car, a local tour or a walking holiday works better than trying to rely on buses.

## 4. Oxford

Oxford's colleges, libraries and quadrangles have been the setting for countless books and films. Visit the Bodleian Library, Christ Church and the Ashmolean Museum, and climb a tower for the view over the "dreaming spires".

**Tip** Each college sets its own opening hours, and many close during exams. Check before you go.

## 5. Stonehenge

The stone circle on Salisbury Plain was built in stages around 5,000 years ago, and nobody is completely sure why. Visits start at a visitor centre with a good exhibition, and a shuttle takes you to the stones. Combine it with the city of Salisbury, whose cathedral has the tallest spire in Britain.

**Tip** Tickets are timed and cheaper when booked online in advance with English Heritage.

## 6. York

York was founded by the Romans, ruled by the Vikings and rebuilt by the medieval church. Walk the city walls, visit York Minster, one of the largest Gothic cathedrals in northern Europe, and get lost in the timber-framed streets of the Shambles.

**Tip** York is under two hours from London by fast train, and a good stop on the way to Scotland.

## 7. The Lake District

England's largest national park, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is a landscape of lakes, fells and stone villages that inspired Wordsworth and Beatrix Potter. Take a boat on Windermere or Ullswater and walk at least one fell; Catbells, above Derwentwater, is a classic short climb with a huge view.

**Tip** It rains a lot here. Good shoes and waterproofs make the difference between a great day and a miserable one.

## 8. Cornwall

England's southwest tip has a coastline of cliffs, coves and fishing villages, with water that looks almost Mediterranean on a sunny day. St Ives, Mousehole and Padstow are favourites, and the South West Coast Path runs along the whole coast.

**Tip** Cornish roads are narrow and summer traffic is heavy. Try to visit in May, June or September.

## 9. Liverpool

Liverpool is the city of the Beatles, but it is also one of the most interesting cities in the north, with a handsome waterfront, excellent free museums and two cathedrals. Football fans come for Anfield and Goodison Park.

**Tip** The Beatles Story museum and the Cavern Club are on most visitors' lists; the free Museum of Liverpool and the Walker Art Gallery are worth the time too.

## 10. Canterbury

Canterbury Cathedral, where Archbishop Thomas Becket was murdered in 1170, has been a place of pilgrimage for centuries. The medieval centre is compact and lively, and the white cliffs of Dover are close by.

**Tip** High-speed trains from London St Pancras take under an hour, making it an easy day trip.

## Planning your trip

**Entry.** Most visitors who don't need a visa for the UK, including travellers from the US, the EU and Brazil, now need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) before they travel. Check the official rules and apply only on the [UK government website](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-electronic-travel-authorisation-eta).

**Trains.** Advance tickets are often much cheaper than buying on the day. Railcards can cut fares further if you travel a lot.

**Plugs and driving.** The UK uses three-pin Type G plugs, and cars drive on the left.

**Tipping.** A tip of around 10 to 12.5 percent is usual in restaurants when service isn't already included. Check the bill first.
