Indoor cats are safer from traffic, fights and many diseases, but they also live in a much smaller, more predictable world. Without enough to do, some cats become bored, overweight, stressed, or "naughty", which is often just a cat inventing its own entertainment.

You don't need expensive furniture to fix this. The ideas below are built around the [AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1098612X13477537), written by feline veterinary experts. They describe five "pillars" every cat needs:

1. A safe place
2. Multiple, separated key resources (food, water, litter, scratching, play and resting areas)
3. Opportunity for play and predatory behavior
4. Positive, consistent and predictable human interaction
5. An environment that respects the cat's sense of smell

## Pillar 1: A safe place

**1. A hideout in every main room.** A box on its side with a towel in it counts. For a sturdier version, build a [one-box cat house](/diy-cardboard-cat-house/).

**2. Somewhere high.** Clear the top of a bookshelf, or add a stable box tower like our [cardboard cat castle](/cardboard-cat-castle/). Cats feel safer watching from above.

## Pillar 2: Separated resources

**3. Split up food, water and litter.** Cats generally prefer not to eat next to where they drink or go to the toilet. Moving the water bowl a few steps away from the food is free.

**4. More than one of everything in multi-cat homes.** A common guideline is one resource per cat plus one extra, in different locations, so no cat can block another from reaching it.

## Pillar 3: Play and "hunting"

**5. Two short play sessions a day.** A wand toy moved like prey (along the floor, behind furniture, stopping and starting) is the best cat toy there is. Five to ten minutes at a time is enough for many cats.

**6. End with a "catch".** Let your cat catch the toy at the end, then offer a small meal or treat. This mirrors the hunt, catch, eat sequence.

**7. Food puzzles.** Make your cat work for part of their daily food with our [5 DIY cat food puzzles](/diy-cat-food-puzzles/).

**8. Scratching spots.** Put a scratcher near sleeping areas and room entrances. Our [cardboard scratcher guide](/how-to-make-a-cardboard-cat-scratcher/) shows how to make one in an hour.

**9. Toy rotation.** Put most toys away and bring out two or three different ones each week. Old toys feel new again.

## Pillar 4: Predictable human interaction

**10. Let your cat choose.** Many cats prefer short, frequent interactions that they start themselves. Offer a hand for your cat to sniff and rub against rather than reaching straight for them, and stroke the head and cheeks, where most cats like it best.

**11. Keep routines.** Feeding, play and quiet time at roughly the same times each day help cats feel secure.

## Pillar 5: Smell

**12. Respect your cat's scent.** Cats mark their territory by rubbing and scratching, so avoid washing all their bedding at once and avoid strong air fresheners, scented litter and essential oils (many are toxic to cats). You can add good smells instead: a cardboard box from the grocery store, a pinch of catnip or silver vine, or a pot of cat grass.

## Extra: a window with a view

A spot by a window where your cat can watch birds and people is free entertainment. Put a box or folded blanket on the windowsill and make sure the window is secure or screened.

## Signs your cat needs more enrichment

- Overgrooming or bald patches
- Destructive scratching or chewing
- Night-time "zoomies" and waking you up
- Attention-seeking behavior, like knocking things over
- Weight gain and inactivity
- Tension or fights between cats in the same home

Many of these can also have medical causes, so if your cat's behavior changes suddenly, start with a vet check.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much play does an indoor cat need?** There is no single number, because age, health and personality all matter. Many cats do well with two or three short sessions of five to fifteen minutes a day. Kittens and young cats usually need more; senior cats may prefer gentler, shorter games.

**My cat ignores toys. What am I doing wrong?** Usually the toy is moving like a toy, not like prey. Move the wand away from your cat rather than toward them, let it disappear behind furniture, and pause often. Also try different times of day: many cats are most playful around dawn and dusk.

**Is a second cat the answer to boredom?** Not always. Some cats enjoy a companion, but others find another cat stressful, and a new cat means more resources to share. Try enrichment first, and think carefully about your current cat's personality before adopting another.

**Do I need to buy a cat tree?** No. A cleared bookshelf, a windowsill with a folded blanket, or a stable stack of boxes can give your cat height. If you do buy one, choose a heavy, stable model, since a wobbly cat tree can put a cat off climbing altogether.

## Start small

You don't need to do all twelve. Pick one idea from each pillar this week, watch how your cat responds, and build from there.
