France receives more international visitors than any other country, and most of them see only Paris. That is a shame, because the country's high-speed trains make it easy to add a second region. Two or three of the places below, connected by TGV, make a far richer trip than a week in the capital alone.

## 1. Paris

The museums alone could take a week: the Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay and its Impressionists, the Orangerie with Monet's water lilies. Notre-Dame reopened in December 2024 after the fire, and the Sainte-Chapelle's stained glass is one of the great sights of the city. Leave time for simply walking, especially along the Seine and through the Marais.

**Tip** Book the Louvre, the Eiffel Tower summit and Sainte-Chapelle online with a time slot. It saves hours in queues.

## 2. Versailles

The palace of Louis XIV is the most famous royal residence in Europe: the Hall of Mirrors, the State Apartments and gardens so large that you can rent a bike or a rowing boat to explore them. It is an easy half-day from central Paris by train.

**Tip** The palace is closed on Mondays and very crowded on Tuesdays. Arrive at opening time.

## 3. Mont-Saint-Michel

A medieval abbey on a rocky island, surrounded at high tide by the sea. The bay has some of the strongest tides in Europe, and watching the water come in is part of the visit. The village climbing the rock is busy by day and almost empty at night.

**Tip** Stay overnight nearby and visit early or late. Never walk across the sands without a licensed guide: the tide and quicksand are dangerous.

## 4. The Loire Valley

The Loire is lined with hundreds of châteaux built by French kings and nobles. Chambord is the most spectacular, Chenonceau spans a river, and the gardens of Villandry are the most beautiful. The region is flat and has good cycling routes between castles and vineyards.

**Tip** Base yourself in Amboise, Blois or Tours, and pick two or three châteaux. Trying to see more in a day is exhausting.

## 5. Normandy's D-Day beaches

The beaches where Allied troops landed on 6 June 1944, with names like Omaha and Juno, are lined with museums, memorials and cemeteries. The American Cemetery above Omaha Beach is deeply moving. Bayeux, with its 11th-century tapestry, is the best base.

**Tip** A guided tour is worth it here. The stories make the landscape make sense.

## 6. Provence

Hilltop villages, markets, Roman ruins and, in early summer, lavender fields. Visit the Roman theatre in Orange and the Pont du Gard aqueduct, the papal palace in Avignon, and villages such as Gordes and Roussillon in the Luberon.

**Tip** Lavender usually flowers from late June to July, and harvest starts soon after. Check local reports before planning a trip around it.

## 7. The French Riviera

The Côte d'Azur runs from Marseille to the Italian border, with Nice, Cannes, Antibes and Monaco along the way. Nice is the easiest base: its old town, markets and seafront promenade are lovely, and trains run along the coast to the perched village of Èze and to Monaco.

**Tip** July and August are crowded and expensive. Late May, June and September are warm and calmer.

## 8. Chamonix and Mont Blanc

Chamonix sits at the foot of Mont Blanc, the highest mountain in Western Europe. The Aiguille du Midi cable car rises to almost 3,850 metres, with views across the Alps. In summer, there are hundreds of kilometres of hiking trails; in winter, world-class skiing.

**Tip** The weather at altitude can change fast. Check the summit webcams before you buy a cable car ticket.

## 9. Lyon

Lyon is considered by many to be the food capital of France. Eat at a traditional *bouchon*, explore the Renaissance lanes and secret passageways (*traboules*) of Vieux Lyon, and walk up to the basilica of Fourvière for the view.

**Tip** Lyon is two hours from Paris by TGV, and a good stop on the way to the Alps or Provence.

## 10. Alsace

On the border with Germany, Alsace has half-timbered villages, a wine route and some of the most charming towns in France. Strasbourg's cathedral and the canals of its Petite France district are beautiful, and Colmar looks like a fairytale. In December, the region's Christmas markets are among the oldest in Europe.

**Tip** The Christmas markets are magical and extremely busy. Visit on weekdays if you can.

## Planning your trip

**Entry.** France is in the Schengen Area. Entry rules depend on your nationality; check the [official French tourism website](https://www.france.fr/en/) and your government's travel advice before you go.

**Trains.** TGV high-speed trains connect Paris with Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux, Strasbourg and more. The cheapest fares go early; book as soon as your dates are fixed.

**Closures.** Many national museums close one day a week, usually Monday or Tuesday. Check before you plan your days.

**Restaurants.** Service is included in the bill by law. Leaving a little extra for good service is appreciated but not expected.
