A tired dog is usually a well-behaved dog, but exercise is only half of it. Mental work, like sniffing out food, figuring out how to get into something, and ripping open a "present", tires dogs out in a different way, and it is especially useful on days when long walks are not possible.

Puzzle toys from pet stores can be expensive and some dogs solve them in seconds. Cardboard puzzles cost nothing, you can make them harder or easier in a minute, and destroying them is part of the fun.

## Safety rules before you start

Cardboard puzzles are for **supervised play**. Read these first:

- **Shredding is fine; eating is not.** Most dogs tear cardboard apart and leave the pieces. If your dog swallows large pieces, choose rubber puzzle toys instead. Our guide [Can dogs eat cardboard?](/can-dogs-eat-cardboard/) explains the risks and the warning signs.
- **Strip every box.** Remove staples, plastic tape, labels and plastic windows.
- **Use only safe treats.** Never use chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, or anything containing xylitol.
- **Count the calories.** Use part of your dog's daily kibble rather than adding extra treats.
- **One dog per puzzle.** Food games can trigger guarding between dogs. Separate them.

## 1. The muffin tin and balls (beginner)

Not cardboard, but a classic first puzzle. Put a piece of food in some cups of a muffin tin and cover every cup with a tennis ball or a crumpled ball of paper. Your dog has to lift or knock the balls off to find the food.

**Level up:** put food under only a few of the balls.

## 2. The paper roll treat tube

Put a few pieces of kibble inside a toilet paper roll and fold both ends in. Hand it to your dog.

Some dogs carefully unfold one end. Others just rip it apart. Both are correct.

**Level up:** wrap the tube in a sheet of plain packing paper and twist the ends like a candy.

## 3. The egg carton surprise

Use a clean cardboard egg carton without egg residue. Drop treats into a few cups, add crumpled paper on top, close the lid and let your dog open it.

This suits small and medium dogs. A large dog may flatten it in one bite, so give them the box-in-a-box version below.

## 4. Box in a box (the "present")

Put treats into a small box, close it, then place it inside a bigger box filled with crumpled paper. Close the big box loosely the first few times.

Your dog has to dig through the paper, find the small box, and get it open. Many dogs love this one because it combines sniffing, digging and shredding.

**Level up:** add a third box, or tape the flaps with a small piece of paper tape.

## 5. The paper roll grid

Stand toilet paper rolls upright inside a shoe box so they fit snugly. Drop treats into some of the rolls. Your dog has to pull rolls out, or push their nose in, to get the food.

**Level up:** put a crumpled piece of paper on top of each roll as a plug.

## 6. The snuffle box

Fill a box with crumpled paper, toilet rolls and strips of fabric, then scatter kibble through it. Dogs use their noses to search every corner. It is such a good calming activity that we wrote a full guide to the [DIY snuffle box](/diy-snuffle-box-for-dogs/).

## How to teach your dog to play

1. **Make the first try easy.** Let your dog watch you put food in, and leave the puzzle open.
2. **Encourage, don't help too much.** Cheer your dog on. If they get stuck for more than a minute, make the puzzle easier rather than solving it for them.
3. **Use a cue.** Say "Find it!" as you put the puzzle down. Your dog will learn that it means a game is starting.
4. **Clean up together.** Pick up the shredded cardboard when the game ends, so it does not turn into a chew toy.

## How often?

One or two short puzzle sessions a day is a good start. Puzzles work especially well:

- at mealtimes, instead of a bowl;
- before you leave the house;
- on rainy days or when your dog is recovering and cannot go on long walks (check with your vet about activity limits);
- when visitors arrive and your dog needs something else to focus on.

For more ideas when you are stuck inside, see [10 indoor games for dogs on rainy days](/indoor-games-for-dogs/).

## When to skip cardboard puzzles

- Dogs that swallow cardboard rather than shredding it
- Puppies who are teething and chew everything (use rubber toys made for puppies)
- Dogs with food guarding problems around people or other pets (talk to a qualified trainer or veterinary behaviorist)

Every dog is different. Watch your dog the first few times, and adjust the puzzle to their style of play.
