Many dogs love to destroy cardboard boxes, and we use cardboard for several of the dog puzzles on this site. So it is fair to ask: what happens if your dog actually eats some?

The short answer: small amounts of plain cardboard are usually not a problem, but large amounts, or cardboard with tape, staples or food residue, can be. Here is how to tell the difference.

> **If your dog has eaten a large amount of cardboard, or is showing any of the warning signs below, call your veterinarian now.** Do not try to make your dog vomit unless a vet tells you to.

## Is cardboard toxic to dogs?

Plain brown cardboard is made mostly of wood fiber and is not considered toxic. The concern is not poisoning. The concern is the **physical** effect of swallowing something your dog cannot digest.

What *can* make cardboard dangerous is what is on it or in it:

- **Staples and metal clips**, which can injure the mouth or gut
- **Plastic tape and labels**, which do not break down
- **Food residue**, especially if the box held something toxic to dogs, like chocolate, grapes, onions or products containing xylitol
- **Chemical residue**, from boxes that held cleaning products, pesticides or medication
- **Glossy coatings and heavy printing**, which add materials your dog does not need to eat

## How much is too much?

There is no exact number, because it depends on the size of your dog and the size of the pieces.

- **A few small pieces** from a dog who shredded a box usually pass through on their own. Watch your dog for the next few days.
- **A lot of cardboard**, large chunks, or a small dog who ate a big piece is more worrying, because it can cause a **gastrointestinal obstruction** (a blockage).
- **Cardboard swallowed with tape, string or plastic** is more worrying still.

When in doubt, call your vet and describe how much your dog ate and how big your dog is. They can tell you whether to watch at home or come in.

## Warning signs of a blockage

Contact your veterinarian or an emergency clinic right away if your dog shows any of these after eating cardboard:

- Repeated vomiting, or trying to vomit without bringing anything up
- Not eating, or refusing water
- Lethargy or weakness
- A painful or bloated belly (your dog may hunch, whine, or not want to be touched)
- Straining to poop, or no poop for more than a day or two
- Diarrhea, especially with blood

Signs can appear within hours, or a few days later.

In the United States, if you are worried that something your dog swallowed was toxic, you can also call the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control) at (888) 426-4435. A fee may apply.

## Why do dogs eat cardboard?

**Chewing and shredding** cardboard is very normal. It is satisfying, it feels good on the teeth, and it often comes after a fun moment like a delivery arriving.

**Eating** it, though, can have several causes:

- **Boredom or excess energy**, especially in young, active breeds
- **Teething** in puppies
- **Anxiety**, such as when left alone
- **The smell of food** on the box
- **Pica**, the medical name for eating non-food items. It can sometimes be linked to medical issues, so a dog that repeatedly eats non-food items should be checked by a vet.

## How to stop a dog from eating cardboard

1. **Pick up boxes right away.** Flatten them and store them where your dog cannot reach.
2. **Supervise cardboard play.** Let your dog shred during a puzzle game, then clear everything away.
3. **Trade, don't chase.** If your dog grabs a box, offer a treat or a toy in exchange. Chasing can turn it into a game of keep-away.
4. **Give better chew options**, like durable rubber toys and appropriate chews.
5. **Add more enrichment.** Sniffing games, puzzles and training sessions burn mental energy. Try the [DIY snuffle box](/diy-snuffle-box-for-dogs/) or [indoor games for rainy days](/indoor-games-for-dogs/).
6. **Teach "drop it" and "leave it".** Practice with low-value items first, and reward generously.
7. **Talk to your vet** if the habit is sudden, intense, or comes with other changes in behavior or health.

## Should I still give my dog cardboard puzzles?

For most dogs, yes. Shredding a box to get to a treat is great fun and good enrichment. The key is to:

- use clean boxes with no tape or staples;
- supervise every session;
- clean up afterward;
- switch to rubber puzzle toys if your dog starts swallowing pieces.
