Spain is one of the easiest countries in Europe to travel around. High-speed trains link most of the big cities, the food is good almost everywhere, and the days are long. It also has strong regional identities: Catalonia, the Basque Country and Andalusia can feel like different countries.

The rhythm takes some getting used to. Lunch is rarely before 2 p.m., and dinner often starts at 9 or later.

## 1. Barcelona

Barcelona is Gaudí's city. The Sagrada Família, still under construction more than a century after work began, is one of the most extraordinary buildings in the world. Add Park Güell, Casa Batlló, the narrow streets of the Gothic Quarter and an evening on the beach at Barceloneta.

**Tip** Tickets for the Sagrada Família and Park Güell are timed and often sell out. Book them on the official websites as soon as your dates are fixed.

## 2. Madrid

Madrid has three of the great art museums of Europe within walking distance of each other: the Prado, the Reina Sofía (home to Picasso's *Guernica*) and the Thyssen-Bornemisza. Between museums, walk through Retiro Park and the Plaza Mayor, and save energy for the city's late nights.

**Tip** The Prado and the Reina Sofía offer free entry in the last hours of most days. Expect queues.

## 3. Seville

Seville is the capital of Andalusia, and in spring it is at its best: orange blossom in the streets, the Holy Week processions and the April Fair. Visit the Real Alcázar palace, the cathedral and its Giralda tower, and the vast Plaza de España, then watch flamenco in the Triana neighbourhood.

**Tip** Summer in Seville is extremely hot, often above 40 °C. Spring and autumn are far more pleasant.

## 4. Granada and the Alhambra

The Alhambra, a palace and fortress complex built by the Nasrid dynasty, is the most visited monument in Spain and the finest example of Islamic architecture in Western Europe. The city below it is just as rewarding: the white streets of the Albaicín and the view of the Alhambra from the Mirador de San Nicolás at sunset.

**Tip** Entry to the Nasrid Palaces is limited to a specific time slot, and tickets sell out weeks ahead in high season. Buy them only from the official Alhambra website or authorised sellers.

## 5. Córdoba

Córdoba's Mezquita-Catedral began as a mosque in the 8th century, with a forest of red-and-white striped arches, and later had a cathedral built in its middle. It is one of the most unusual buildings anywhere. In May, the city's private courtyards fill with flowers for the Patios Festival.

**Tip** Córdoba is an easy day trip from Seville or Madrid by high-speed train, but staying one night lets you see it without the day-trip crowds.

## 6. Toledo

Toledo sits on a hill almost surrounded by the Tagus River, and for centuries it was a city where Christian, Muslim and Jewish communities lived side by side. Its cathedral, synagogues and steep lanes are best explored on foot. El Greco lived and painted here.

**Tip** High-speed trains from Madrid take about half an hour. Stay for the evening, after the day-trippers leave.

## 7. Valencia

Valencia is where paella comes from, and it combines a beautiful old centre with the futuristic City of Arts and Sciences. The Turia Gardens, built in an old riverbed, run across the city and are perfect for cycling. In March, the city explodes with the Fallas festival.

**Tip** Real Valencian paella is a lunch dish. Locals rarely eat it at dinner.

## 8. San Sebastián

San Sebastián, or Donostia in Basque, has a perfect curved beach, La Concha, and one of the highest concentrations of Michelin-starred restaurants in the world. For most visitors, though, the real attraction is the bars of the old town and their pintxos, small dishes lined up on the counter.

**Tip** Order one or two pintxos and a drink in each bar, then move on. That is how locals do it.

## 9. Mallorca

The largest of the Balearic Islands has much more than resorts. The Serra de Tramuntana mountains, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, hide stone villages like Valldemossa and Deià, and the coast has dozens of small coves with clear water.

**Tip** July and August are very busy. May, June and September have warm sea and fewer people.

## 10. The Canary Islands

Off the coast of Africa, the Canaries are warm all year. Tenerife has Mount Teide, the highest peak in Spain, in a national park of volcanic landscapes. Lanzarote has black lava fields and architecture by César Manrique; La Palma and La Gomera are greener and quieter.

**Tip** Visiting the summit of Teide requires a free permit, which is limited and should be booked well in advance through the national park.

## Planning your trip

**Entry.** Spain is in the Schengen Area. Entry rules depend on your nationality; check the [official Spanish tourism website](https://www.spain.info/en/) and your government's travel advice before you go.

**Trains.** High-speed trains connect Madrid with Barcelona, Seville, Córdoba, Málaga, Valencia and more. Tickets are usually cheaper when booked early.

**Book ahead.** The Alhambra, the Sagrada Família and Park Güell need advance tickets in almost every season.

**Tipping.** Service is not usually added to the bill, and tipping is modest: rounding up or leaving a few euros for good service is normal.
