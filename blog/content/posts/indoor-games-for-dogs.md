Rain, snow, heat waves, an injury, or a day when you simply cannot get out: sooner or later, every dog owner faces a day when the usual walk is not happening. A dog with energy and nothing to do usually finds its own entertainment, and it may involve your shoes.

These ten games use things you already have at home. Mix two or three of them and most dogs will be ready for a nap.

## Before you start

- **Clear some space** and move anything breakable.
- **Use part of your dog's daily food** as rewards, so the games do not add extra calories.
- **Keep sessions short.** Five to ten minutes of focused mental work is surprisingly tiring for a dog.
- **Check with your vet first** if your dog is recovering from surgery or has movement restrictions.

## 1. Find it

The simplest scent game. Have your dog sit and stay (or have someone hold them), let them watch you place a treat a few steps away, then say "Find it!"

Once they understand the game, hide treats out of sight: behind a chair leg, under the edge of a rug, on a low step. Then hide them in another room.

## 2. The cup game

Place three plastic cups upside down. Let your dog watch you put a treat under one, shuffle slowly, and ask your dog to choose. Reward a nose or paw on the right cup.

Most dogs win by smell rather than by following the cups, which is perfectly fine.

## 3. Hide and seek

Ask your dog to stay, or have someone hold them, then go hide in another room. Call their name once. When they find you, celebrate with a treat or a toy.

This game is great practice for recall, too.

## 4. The cardboard shred box

Put a few treats into a box with crumpled paper, close it loosely, and let your dog rip it open. It is noisy, messy and very satisfying for dogs who love to destroy things. Supervise and clean up afterward. See our [cardboard puzzle toys](/diy-dog-puzzle-toys-from-cardboard/) for more versions.

## 5. Snuffle box mealtime

Instead of a bowl, serve dinner in a [DIY snuffle box](/diy-snuffle-box-for-dogs/). A meal that normally lasts 30 seconds can turn into 10 minutes of sniffing.

## 6. Learn a new trick

Short training sessions are excellent mental exercise. Easy tricks to teach indoors:

- **Touch**: your dog touches your palm with their nose
- **Spin**: your dog turns in a circle following a treat in your hand
- **Paw** or **high five**
- **Go to mat**: your dog goes to lie on a towel or bed

Use small rewards and stop while your dog is still having fun.

## 7. Gentle tug

Tug is a great energy burner when it has rules. Your dog grabs the toy when you say "Take it", lets go when you say "Drop it", and the game pauses if teeth touch skin. Keep the toy low, pull side to side rather than up and down, and let your dog win sometimes.

## 8. The name game

Teach your dog the names of their toys. Start with one toy: say "Ball!" each time you play with it. Then put it next to a second toy and ask for "Ball". Some dogs learn the names of many toys, and the learning process itself is tiring.

## 9. Stair or hallway fetch

If your dog is healthy and the floor is not slippery, a soft toy rolled down a hallway gives a short burst of activity. Avoid fetch on stairs for puppies, older dogs, and dogs with joint problems, and put a rug down on slippery floors.

## 10. Calm chew time

After active games, help your dog settle with something to chew or lick for a few minutes, such as a suitable chew toy or a lick mat with a thin layer of plain dog-safe food. Licking and chewing help many dogs relax.

## Putting it together

A simple rainy-day plan:

1. **Morning:** breakfast in a snuffle box
2. **Midday:** 5 minutes of trick training plus a round of hide and seek
3. **Afternoon:** find-it with treats hidden around the house
4. **Evening:** gentle tug, then calm chew time

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should indoor play last?** It depends on your dog's age, breed and energy, but mental games are more tiring than they look. Several sessions of five to ten minutes, spread across the day, usually work better than one long session.

**My dog gets overexcited indoors. What helps?** Choose calmer games, like find-it, the cup game and snuffle boxes, over chase and tug. Finish every session with a quiet activity such as chewing or licking, and keep the energy low yourself.

**Are these games safe for puppies?** Most are, with shorter sessions and very easy versions. Skip stair and hallway fetch on slippery floors, and supervise closely with cardboard and paper, since puppies tend to chew and swallow more than adult dogs.

**Can I use these games for a dog on crate rest?** Some calm scent and licking games may be suitable, but crate rest after surgery or injury has strict limits. Ask your veterinarian which activities are allowed before you start.

## Not a replacement for walks

None of this fully replaces walks and time outside, which matter for your dog's physical health and social life. But it can turn a frustrating day stuck at home into a good one.
