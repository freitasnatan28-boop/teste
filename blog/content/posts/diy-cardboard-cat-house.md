If your cat already climbs into every box that arrives, a cat house is just that box with better architecture. This version uses one box, takes under an hour, and needs only a knife, tape and something soft for the floor.

The goal is not just a cute shape. A good hideout gives a cat a place to rest out of sight, a way to watch the room, and a clear exit. Those details are what make a cat actually use it.

## Why a hideout matters

Cats are both predators and prey animals. When they feel unsure, they look for an enclosed spot where they can see without being seen. Researchers at Utrecht University found that newly arrived shelter cats given a [hiding box adapted to their new environment faster](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2014.09.002) than cats without one. Your living room is not a shelter, but the same need applies when there are visitors, a new pet, loud noises or a move. We explain more in [why cats love boxes](/why-do-cats-love-boxes/).

## Choosing the box

Pick a box your cat can turn around and lie stretched out in:

| Cat size | Minimum box size |
|---|---|
| Kitten or small cat | 14 × 10 × 10 in (35 × 25 × 25 cm) |
| Average adult cat | 16 × 12 × 12 in (40 × 30 × 30 cm) |
| Large cat (Maine Coon, etc.) | 20 × 16 × 16 in (50 × 40 × 40 cm) |

Use a sturdy, clean, single- or double-wall corrugated box with all four top flaps intact. Remove staples, plastic tape and labels first.

## What you need

- One box (see sizes above)
- Utility knife and a pencil
- A round object about 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm) across to trace for the door, such as a plate or a bowl
- Paper tape or kraft tape (or a little PVA glue)
- An old T-shirt, towel or fleece for the floor

## Step 1: Mark the door

Choose one of the shorter sides as the front. Trace a circle 7 to 8 inches (18 to 20 cm) wide, positioned so the bottom of the circle sits **at least 2 inches (5 cm) above the floor**. That lip keeps the bedding inside and keeps the front panel strong.

For large cats, go up to 9 inches (23 cm). The door should let your cat walk in without squeezing, but it should still feel enclosed.

![Diagram of the cat house front panel with door measurements and the finished house with a peaked roof](/assets/img/cat-house-diagram.svg "A 7–8 inch door with a 2 inch lip, plus side windows and a folded roof.")

## Step 2: Cut the door and windows

Cut the door with several shallow passes of the knife. Then add one or two windows on the long sides, about 2 by 2 inches (5 by 5 cm), at the height of your cat's head when lying down.

Windows are not just decoration. A cat inside can watch what is coming without leaving, and many cats prefer a hideout with a second view. If your cat is nervous, you can make one window big enough to work as an emergency exit.

Smooth any rough edges by running the back of a spoon along them.

## Step 3: Build the roof

Stand the box up with the flaps on top. Take the two long flaps and lean them toward each other so they meet in the middle, forming a peak. Tape them together along the top with paper tape.

Next, fold the two short end flaps up into triangles to close the gable ends: trim each into a triangle that matches the slope of the roof, then tape it to the roof edges from the inside.

If your cat likes to sit **on top** of things, skip the peak. Tape the flaps flat to make a lookout platform instead, and add a second layer of cardboard on top for strength.

## Step 4: Add the bedding

Fold an old T-shirt or fleece and place it inside. Something that already smells like you or your cat works better than a new blanket.

## Step 5: Decorate (optional)

If you want to paint the outside, use a water-based, non-toxic paint and let it dry for several days before your cat uses the house. Do not paint the inside, the door edges or anything your cat might chew. Our [guide to safe glue, paint and tape](/safe-glue-paint-and-tape-for-pet-diy/) lists what to use and what to avoid.

## Where to put it

Location makes or breaks a cat house:

- **Quiet, but not isolated.** A corner of a room the family uses works better than a closed spare room.
- **Door facing the room**, with a wall behind, so your cat can see who is coming.
- **Away from the food bowl and litter box.**
- **Somewhere warm**, such as near a sunny window. Cats like warmer temperatures than people do.

Leave it alone for a few days. Many cats investigate a new object at night, when no one is watching.

## Safety notes

- Check the house regularly. Once it gets crushed or chewed, recycle it and make a new one.
- If your cat chews and swallows cardboard, this is not the right project for them. Use a fabric cat bed instead and mention the chewing to your vet.
- Keep the house away from heaters, candles and stovetops.
