Italy has more UNESCO World Heritage Sites than any other country, and the temptation is to see all of them. Resist it. The classic first trip of Rome, Florence and Venice already involves a lot of moving. A better plan is to add one region where you can slow down, such as Tuscany, the lakes or the coast.

## 1. Rome

Rome has nearly three thousand years of history on display at once. In a few days you can see the Colosseum and the Roman Forum, the Pantheon, St Peter's Basilica and the Sistine Chapel in the Vatican Museums. In between, the evenings in Trastevere and the piazzas of the centre are as memorable as the monuments.

**Tip** Book the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums online with a time slot. Without a booking, you can lose half a day in queues.

## 2. Florence

The city where the Renaissance began has Michelangelo's *David* at the Accademia, the Uffizi Gallery, and Brunelleschi's dome over the cathedral, which you can climb. Cross the Ponte Vecchio and walk up to Piazzale Michelangelo for the classic view over the rooftops.

**Tip** The climb up the cathedral dome requires a timed reservation. It has more than 400 steps and no lift.

## 3. Venice

There is nowhere else like it: a city of canals, with no cars, built on islands in a lagoon. Ride a vaporetto (water bus) down the Grand Canal, visit St Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace, and then get lost in the quiet back streets of Cannaregio and Castello, where most visitors never go.

**Tip** Venice has been charging day-trippers an access fee on its busiest days. Check the city's official website for the current dates and rules before you visit.

## 4. The Amalfi Coast

Towns such as Positano, Amalfi and Ravello cling to cliffs above the sea on this stretch of coast south of Naples. The views are spectacular from the road and even better from the water. The Path of the Gods, a trail high above the coast, is one of the most beautiful walks in Italy.

**Tip** The coastal road is narrow and jammed in summer. Ferries between the towns are often faster and more pleasant than buses.

## 5. Cinque Terre

Five colourful fishing villages are tucked into the cliffs of the Ligurian coast and linked by trains and hiking trails. The area is a national park, and some of the trails between the villages require a park card.

**Tip** Base yourself in nearby La Spezia or Levanto, and visit early in the morning, before the day trips arrive.

## 6. Tuscany

Beyond Florence, Tuscany is a landscape of vineyards, cypress-lined roads and hill towns. Siena has one of the most beautiful squares in Europe, the Piazza del Campo. San Gimignano has its medieval towers, and the Val d'Orcia has the rolling hills from countless photographs.

**Tip** A car makes the countryside much easier to explore. Be careful of ZTL zones (limited traffic areas) in town centres, where driving without a permit leads to fines.

## 7. Pompeii and Naples

The Roman town buried by Vesuvius in AD 79 is the most complete picture of everyday Roman life anywhere. Nearby, Herculaneum is smaller and better preserved. Naples itself is chaotic, lively and the home of pizza; its National Archaeological Museum holds many of Pompeii's treasures.

**Tip** Pompeii is huge and shaded spots are rare. Bring water and a hat, and plan for at least half a day.

## 8. Lake Como

Lake Como, at the foot of the Alps, is surrounded by elegant villas and gardens. Bellagio, Varenna and Menaggio are linked by ferries, and Villa del Balbianello and Villa Carlotta are the gardens to visit. Milan is only an hour away by train.

**Tip** Buy a day ticket for the ferries in the central part of the lake. Hopping between towns by boat is the best part of a visit.

## 9. The Dolomites

These jagged limestone peaks in the north are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most dramatic mountain landscapes in Europe. The Tre Cime di Lavaredo, the Seceda ridge and the Alpe di Siusi meadows are favourites for hiking in summer; in winter, the area is famous for skiing.

**Tip** Some of the most popular roads and viewpoints now have tolls, parking limits or reservations in high season. Check the local tourist office before you go.

## 10. Sicily

The largest island in the Mediterranean has Greek temples at Agrigento, the mosaics of Monreale, Baroque towns such as Noto, and Mount Etna, the most active volcano in Europe. The food is a world of its own: arancini, cannoli, granita and fish straight from the market.

**Tip** Sicily is big. A week is the minimum to see both the west, around Palermo, and the east, around Catania and Taormina.

## Planning your trip

**Entry.** Italy is in the Schengen Area. Entry rules depend on your nationality; check the [official Italian tourism website](https://www.italia.it/en) and your government's travel advice before you go.

**Trains.** High-speed trains connect Rome, Florence, Bologna, Milan, Venice and Naples. Regional trains are cheap and do not need advance booking, but remember to validate paper tickets before boarding.

**Book ahead.** The Colosseum, the Vatican Museums, the Uffizi, the *Last Supper* in Milan and the Florence dome all need advance reservations in busy periods.

**Coperto.** Many restaurants add a small cover charge (*coperto*) per person. It is normal and is not a tip.
