The United States is less a single destination than a collection of very different ones. A first trip usually works best when it combines one or two cities with one region of national parks, rather than trying to cross the whole country.

This list mixes both. Distances matter: New York to San Francisco is a six-hour flight, and the national parks of the West are often hours from the nearest airport.

## 1. New York City

New York rewards walking more than any other American city. Cross the Brooklyn Bridge on foot, spend an afternoon in Central Park and an evening in a Broadway theatre, and take the free Staten Island Ferry for the classic view of the Statue of Liberty and the Manhattan skyline. The big museums, the Met and MoMA among them, could fill a week on their own.

**Tip** The subway runs around the clock and is the fastest way to get around. You can pay at the turnstile with a contactless card or phone.

## 2. Washington, D.C.

The capital is the best value in the country for museums: most of the Smithsonian's museums on and around the National Mall are free. Walk the Mall from the Capitol to the Lincoln Memorial, especially at dusk, when the monuments are lit.

**Tip** Some popular Smithsonian museums use free timed-entry passes at busy times. Check before you go.

## 3. Grand Canyon National Park

Nothing prepares you for the scale of it. Most visitors go to the South Rim, which is open all year and has the classic viewpoints, lodges and the Rim Trail. Stay for sunset and sunrise, when the colours change minute by minute.

**Tip** Hiking down is far easier than hiking back up. Rangers warn every year against trying to reach the river and return in a single day.

## 4. Yellowstone National Park

The first national park in the world, created in 1872, sits on top of a volcanic hotspot. That is why it has more geysers and hot springs than anywhere else on Earth, including Old Faithful and the rainbow-coloured Grand Prismatic Spring. It is also one of the best places in the country to see bison, elk and, with luck, wolves and bears.

**Tip** Many park roads close from early November to spring. Summer is the main season, so book lodging inside the park months ahead.

## 5. Yosemite National Park

Granite cliffs, giant sequoias and some of the tallest waterfalls in North America, all in the Sierra Nevada of California. Yosemite Valley, with El Capitan and Half Dome, is the heart of the park and gets very busy.

**Tip** In recent years, the park has required reservations to drive in at peak times. Check the current rules on the National Park Service website before you plan your dates.

## 6. San Francisco

Hills, fog, cable cars and the Golden Gate Bridge. Walk or cycle across the bridge, take the ferry to Alcatraz, and eat your way through the Mission District and Chinatown. San Francisco is also the base for trips to the wine country of Napa and Sonoma, and to Yosemite.

**Tip** Summer is often cool and foggy here. Bring layers, even in July.

## 7. New Orleans

No other American city sounds or tastes like New Orleans. Live jazz spills out of the bars on Frenchmen Street, the French Quarter is full of iron balconies, and the food, from beignets to gumbo, is a reason to visit on its own.

**Tip** Mardi Gras is spectacular but expensive and crowded. Spring, with its music festivals and milder weather, is a good alternative.

## 8. Chicago

Chicago has some of the best architecture in the country, and the best way to see it is from the river on an architecture boat tour. Add the Art Institute, deep-dish pizza and a walk along the lakefront.

**Tip** Winters are bitterly cold and windy. Late spring to early autumn is the time to go.

## 9. Zion and the national parks of Utah

Southern Utah has five national parks within a few hours' drive of each other. Zion has the narrow canyon walks; Bryce Canyon has forests of orange rock spires; Arches has more than two thousand natural stone arches. Together with Capitol Reef and Canyonlands, they make one of the great road trips in the world.

**Tip** Summer temperatures can be extreme in the desert. Start hikes early, carry more water than you think you need, and check for flash flood warnings before entering slot canyons.

## 10. Hawaii

Hawaii is six main islands, each with its own character. Oʻahu has Honolulu and Waikīkī; Maui has the road to Hāna and the summit of Haleakalā; the Island of Hawaiʻi, the "Big Island", has active volcanoes in Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park.

**Tip** Pick one or two islands. Moving between them means a flight each time.

## Planning your trip

**Entry.** Citizens of Visa Waiver Program countries need an approved [ESTA](https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/) before travelling. Many other nationalities, including Brazilians, need a visa, and appointment waiting times can be long. Check the rules on the [US State Department website](https://travel.state.gov/) for your country.

**National parks.** An annual "America the Beautiful" pass can save money if you visit several parks. Check entry fees, reservations and closures on the [National Park Service website](https://www.nps.gov/).

**Getting around.** Outside the big cities, you will almost certainly need a car. Public transport to national parks is limited.

**Tipping.** Tipping is expected in restaurants, bars and taxis, and is not included in the price. Plan for it in your budget.

**Health insurance.** Medical care is very expensive in the United States. Do not travel without insurance that covers it.
