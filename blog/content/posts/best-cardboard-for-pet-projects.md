Not all cardboard is the same. A flimsy shipping box that works fine for a quick puzzle toy will fold under a cat jumping onto a castle tower. Knowing a little about how cardboard is made helps you pick the right box for each project, and most of the time you can still get it for free.

## Corrugated cardboard, explained

The brown boxes we call "cardboard" are technically **corrugated fiberboard**. Look at a cut edge and you will see the layers:

- **Liners:** flat sheets of paper on the outside
- **Flutes:** the wavy layer in the middle that gives the board its strength

The flutes run in one direction across the board, a bit like wood grain. That matters in two ways:

1. **Folding:** cardboard folds most cleanly *along* the flutes.
2. **Strength:** a panel is stiffest when you press down along the flutes, which is why boxes are made with the flutes running vertically on their sides.

For a [cardboard cat scratcher](/how-to-make-a-cardboard-cat-scratcher/), you cut *across* the flutes so the wavy edge becomes the scratching surface.

## Single-wall vs double-wall

| Type | What it looks like | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-wall** | One wavy layer between two liners | Puzzle toys, snuffle boxes, scratcher strips, simple hideouts |
| **Double-wall** | Two wavy layers, three liners; noticeably thicker | Cat castles, platforms, anything a pet climbs or stands on |
| **Triple-wall** | Three wavy layers; very rigid | Bases and heavy-duty platforms (rare in households) |

If you are not sure, look at the edge: two wavy layers means double-wall.

## Flute sizes

Flutes come in standard sizes, named with letters. The most common in household boxes:

- **C-flute** (about 4 mm thick): the standard shipping box. A good all-rounder.
- **B-flute** (about 3 mm): common in smaller boxes and retail packaging. Easy to cut, makes fine scratcher strips.
- **E-flute** (about 1.5 mm): thin board used for pizza boxes and some product packaging. Easy to fold into small puzzles, but too weak for structures.
- **BC double-wall** (about 7 mm): a B and a C layer combined. Great for castles and platforms.

You don't need to memorize these. Thicker means stronger and harder to cut.

## Where to find good boxes for free

- **Your own deliveries.** Save the sturdiest boxes and flatten them for storage.
- **Grocery stores and supermarkets.** Ask at customer service or the stockroom; many are happy to give boxes away. Boxes from canned goods are strong and compact.
- **Liquor and wine stores.** Wine boxes are usually sturdy, and the cardboard dividers inside are handy for small projects.
- **Appliance and electronics stores.** Large, heavy-duty boxes from TVs or appliances make excellent castle bases.
- **Furniture stores.** Big flat sheets, perfect for platforms and walls.
- **Local "buy nothing" or recycling groups.** People who have just moved often want to get rid of boxes.

## Boxes to avoid

- **Waxed or plastic-coated boxes.** Many boxes for produce, meat or frozen food are coated to resist moisture. They are slippery, hard to glue, and not something your pet should chew.
- **Glossy, heavily printed packaging.** Use plain brown board where you can.
- **Boxes that held chemicals**, including cleaning products, pesticides, garden products or medication.
- **Boxes with food residue**, especially chocolate, onions, grapes or anything containing xylitol.
- **Damp, stained or moldy boxes.**
- **Boxes with lots of staples or tape.** You can remove these, but it takes time and it is easy to miss a staple.

## Basic tools

You only need a few things:

- **A utility knife** with snap-off or replaceable blades. Change the blade often; a dull blade is a dangerous blade.
- **A metal ruler** to guide straight cuts.
- **A cutting mat or an old board** to protect your table.
- **Scissors** for thin board and paper.
- **A pencil.**
- **Something to score folds**, such as a butter knife or an empty ballpoint pen. Pressing a line before you fold gives a crisp, clean edge.

## Cutting and folding tips

- **Cut in several light passes** rather than one hard one. You get a cleaner edge and the blade is far less likely to slip.
- **Cut along a metal ruler** and keep your other hand on the far side of the ruler, away from the blade's path.
- **Score before you fold.** Press a line with a butter knife or an empty pen, then fold away from the scored side.
- **Fold along the flutes when you can.** Folds across the flutes crack and look ragged; if you must fold across them, score twice, a few millimeters apart.
- **Round off corners** on doors and windows by tracing a coin or a mug. Rounded openings are stronger than sharp corners and gentler on fur.
- **Smooth rough edges** by running the back of a spoon along them.

## Storing cardboard

Flatten boxes and store them somewhere dry, standing on their edge rather than lying flat on a concrete floor where they can absorb moisture. Keep them where your pets can't get to them, both so the boxes stay clean and so your dog doesn't start a shredding party without you.
