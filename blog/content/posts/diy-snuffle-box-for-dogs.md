A snuffle mat is a rubber mat covered in fleece strips where you hide kibble. A snuffle **box** is the cardboard version: a box filled with crumpled paper, rolls and fabric, with food scattered through it. It takes about ten minutes to make and keeps many dogs busy far longer than their usual meal.

<!-- PHOTO: your dog with its nose deep in the snuffle box -->

## Why sniffing matters

A dog's nose is its main way of understanding the world. Letting a dog use it is one of the easiest forms of enrichment there is.

There is research behind this. In a 2019 study, [Let me sniff! Nosework induces positive judgment bias in pet dogs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2018.12.009), dogs that did two weeks of nosework became more optimistic in a behavioral test than dogs that did heelwork training. In other words, sniffing seemed to put them in a better mood.

Sniffing games are also low impact, which makes a snuffle box a good fit for older dogs and for days when a long walk is not possible.

## What you need

- A cardboard box, open at the top. For small dogs, a shoe box. For large dogs, a box roughly 16 × 12 × 6 in (40 × 30 × 15 cm) or bigger.
- Plain packing paper, kraft paper or plain paper (avoid glossy magazines)
- Several empty toilet paper rolls
- Optional: strips cut from an old fleece blanket or cotton T-shirt
- Your dog's kibble or small, safe treats

Remove staples, plastic tape and labels from the box first.

## How to make it

1. **Trim the box** so the sides are about as high as your dog's elbow. Your dog should be able to push its nose into every corner without climbing in.
2. **Crumple the paper** into loose balls and fill the box about two-thirds full.
3. **Tuck in the rolls** standing up and lying down between the paper.
4. **Weave in fabric strips**, if using, between the paper balls.
5. **Scatter the food.** Sprinkle some on top where it is easy to see, and drop the rest down between the paper.

That's it. Put it on the floor and say "Find it!"

## Make it easier or harder

| Level | What to change |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Fewer paper balls, most food on top |
| Intermediate | Fill the box, food mostly hidden |
| Advanced | Put some food inside the toilet rolls with the ends folded closed |
| Expert | Wrap a few treats in paper "candies", then bury them |

Always start easier than you think you need. A dog that finds food quickly learns that the box is worth searching.

## Tips for a better game

- **Use strong-smelling food the first time**, such as a few small pieces of your dog's favorite treat mixed in with kibble.
- **Let your dog work.** Resist pointing at the food.
- **Feed a whole meal this way** on days your dog cannot go out much. It turns a 30-second meal into 10 or 15 minutes of work.
- **Rebuild it every time.** Re-crumple the paper and hide the food in new places so the box stays interesting.

## Safety notes

- **Supervise.** A snuffle box is not a toy to leave with your dog.
- **Watch for eating, not just shredding.** If your dog starts swallowing paper or cardboard, end the game and switch to a washable fabric snuffle mat. Read [Can dogs eat cardboard?](/can-dogs-eat-cardboard/) for warning signs.
- **Separate dogs** that might guard food from each other.
- **Throw it away when it gets dirty.** Cardboard and paper absorb grease and drool, and the box should not become a place where old food sits for days.

## Why not just buy a snuffle mat?

A fabric snuffle mat is washable and lasts longer, so if your dog loves this game, it is worth considering. But a snuffle box is a free way to find out whether your dog enjoys nose games at all, and many dogs love the extra fun of tearing up the paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often can my dog use a snuffle box?** Daily is fine for most dogs, as long as the food comes out of their normal daily amount. Many owners use it for one meal a day.

**Is it good for anxious dogs?** Many owners find sniffing games help their dogs settle, and the study above suggests nosework can improve dogs' mood. A snuffle box is not a treatment for anxiety, though. If your dog is very anxious, talk to your vet or a qualified behavior professional.

**Can puppies use it?** Yes, with close supervision and an easy setup. Puppies are more likely to chew and swallow paper, so end the game as soon as eating starts.

**My dog just tips the box over. Is that cheating?** Not at all. Your dog solved the problem. If you want them to use their nose more, use a heavier, wider box and fill it more loosely so the food sinks down between the paper.
