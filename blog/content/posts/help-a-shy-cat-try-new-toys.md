You spend an afternoon building a [cardboard cat house](/diy-cardboard-cat-house/), and your cat stares at it from across the room as if it might bite. With confident cats, new things get investigated within minutes. Shy cats, newly adopted cats and older cats often need days, and they need the decision to be theirs.

The good news is that a cautious cat who finally decides something is safe often becomes its most loyal user.

## Why new things worry some cats

Cats tend to cope with uncertainty by avoiding it, watching from a distance until they are sure. A new object in their territory smells unfamiliar, changes the layout of a room they know well, and may make noise when touched. For a nervous cat, that is enough to keep away.

Pushing them closer usually backfires. The goal is to make the new thing boring and then, slowly, rewarding.

## Step 1: Make it smell familiar

Smell is how cats decide whether something belongs. Before introducing a new project:

- Rub a soft cloth gently on your cat's cheeks (if they enjoy being stroked there) and then rub it on the new object. Cats leave scent from glands on their cheeks.
- Put a T-shirt you have worn, or your cat's old blanket, inside a new bed or house.
- Avoid strong cleaning products, perfumes and essential oils on or near the project. Many essential oils are toxic to cats.

## Step 2: Put it in a calm, familiar place

Place the new item:

- in a room your cat already uses and feels safe in;
- against a wall, not in the middle of the floor;
- away from doors, noisy appliances and busy walkways;
- with a clear escape route on all sides.

Avoid putting it right next to their bed, food or litter box at first. Your cat should be able to ignore it if they want.

## Step 3: Let them come to it

Then walk away. Many cats investigate new objects at night, when the house is quiet and nobody is watching. Resist the urge to:

- pick your cat up and put them inside;
- push them toward the toy;
- stand and watch closely.

Give it several days before deciding your cat doesn't like it.

## Step 4: Add good things nearby

Once your cat is willing to be in the same room as the object:

- Leave a few treats near it, then on it, then inside it, over several days.
- Sprinkle a pinch of catnip or silver vine on it (see [catnip vs silver vine](/catnip-vs-silver-vine/) if catnip doesn't work for your cat).
- Play a gentle wand toy game near it, letting the toy "hide" behind or inside the object.

## Step 5: Keep play short and gentle

Shy cats often prefer slow, small movements to big, fast ones. Move a wand toy along the floor, let it disappear behind furniture, and stop before your cat becomes overwhelmed. End with a catch and a treat.

Put wand toys away after each session; string is dangerous if swallowed.

## Specific tips by project

- **Scratchers:** place them next to where your cat already scratches. See our [scratcher guide](/how-to-make-a-cardboard-cat-scratcher/).
- **Cat houses:** make the door wide enough and add a window so your cat can see out. A hideout with only one small opening can feel like a trap.
- **Tunnels:** start with a short, straight tunnel with peek holes so it is bright inside. See the [cardboard cat tunnel](/diy-cardboard-cat-tunnel/).
- **Food puzzles:** start with the easiest open-box version. See [5 DIY cat food puzzles](/diy-cat-food-puzzles/).

## For newly adopted cats

New cats usually settle best in a single quiet "base camp" room with food, water, a litter box, a hiding place and a scratcher. A hiding box is especially important in the first days. Research with shelter cats has found that cats given a [hiding box adapted faster](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2014.09.002) to a new environment. Let them explore the rest of the home gradually, when they are ready.

## When shyness is something more

Some cats are simply reserved. But if your cat suddenly becomes much more fearful, hides most of the day, stops eating, or stops using the litter box, see your vet. Pain and illness often show up as changes in behavior. Long-term fearfulness can also be helped by a veterinary behaviorist.

## Patience pays off

It can take a shy cat a week or more to accept something new. Keep the environment calm, let your cat lead, and reward small steps. One day you will find them asleep in the box you built.
