You sprinkle catnip on a new scratcher, and your cat sniffs it once and walks away. That is not unusual. A good share of cats simply do not react to catnip. The good news is that catnip is not the only plant that cats go wild for, and the one that works for many "non-responders" is silver vine.

## What catnip does

Catnip (*Nepeta cataria*) is a plant in the mint family. Its leaves and stems contain **nepetalactone**, a compound that triggers a short burst of behavior in cats that respond to it:

- sniffing, licking and chewing the plant;
- rubbing the head and cheeks on it;
- rolling over, drooling, or kicking with the back legs;
- sometimes a burst of running and play, sometimes a dreamy calm.

The effect usually lasts several minutes, after which most cats lose interest for a while before they can react again. Very young kittens typically do not respond; the reaction usually appears as cats mature.

## Why some cats ignore it

Responding to catnip appears to be largely inherited, so it varies from cat to cat. It is not a sign that something is wrong with your cat, and it does not mean your cat will not enjoy other plants.

## What silver vine is

Silver vine (*Actinidia polygama*) is a climbing plant from the mountains of eastern Asia, related to kiwifruit. In Japan it has a long history as a cat attractant. For cats it is usually sold as dried powder, dried fruit galls, or woody sticks for chewing.

Silver vine contains several compounds related to the active ingredient in catnip, including **nepetalactol**. That may be why many cats respond to it even when catnip leaves them cold.

## What the research found

In 2017, researchers tested 100 domestic cats with four plants and published the results in *BMC Veterinary Research*: [Responsiveness of cats to silver vine, Tatarian honeysuckle, valerian and catnip](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-017-0987-6).

The headline findings:

| Plant | Share of cats that responded |
|---|---|
| Silver vine | about 79% |
| Catnip | about 68% |
| Tatarian honeysuckle | about 53% |
| Valerian root | about 47% |

Most importantly for owners, **most of the cats that did not respond to catnip did respond to silver vine.** So if catnip does nothing for your cat, silver vine is the logical next thing to try.

This was one study of 100 cats, so treat the exact numbers as a guide rather than a guarantee for your own cat.

## Catnip vs silver vine at a glance

| | Catnip | Silver vine |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Europe and Asia, now common worldwide | Eastern Asia |
| Common forms | Dried leaves, sprays, live plants | Powder, dried galls, chew sticks |
| Response rate in the 2017 study | ~68% | ~79% |
| Easy to grow at home? | Yes, in a sunny pot | Rarely grown by owners |
| Typical cost | Low | Slightly higher |

## How to use them in DIY projects

Both work well with cardboard projects, because cardboard holds scent:

- **Scratchers:** rub a pinch of dried catnip or silver vine powder into the grooves of a [cardboard scratcher](/how-to-make-a-cardboard-cat-scratcher/) to draw your cat to it.
- **Hideouts:** put a little under the bedding of a [cardboard cat house](/diy-cardboard-cat-house/) when you first introduce it.
- **Puzzles:** add a tiny pinch to a [food puzzle](/diy-cat-food-puzzles/) to make it more interesting.
- **Kick toys:** fold a small pouch from an old sock, fill it with a little dried catnip, and tie it closed with a tight knot (no string or loose threads).

Use **small amounts**. A pinch is plenty, and less is more for keeping the effect special.

## Is it safe?

Catnip and silver vine are commonly used in cat toys and are generally considered safe in normal amounts. A few practical points:

- **Eating a lot can upset the stomach.** Some cats eat dried catnip; large amounts may cause mild vomiting or diarrhea. Store packets where your cat cannot raid them.
- **Supervise chew sticks.** Throw away silver vine sticks once they splinter or get small enough to swallow.
- **Some cats get over-excited.** A few cats become rough or swat during a catnip high. Give them space, and in multi-cat homes offer it to cats separately if it causes squabbles.
- **Talk to your vet first** if your cat is pregnant, has a medical condition, or is on medication.
- **Never substitute essential oils.** Many essential oils are toxic to cats. Use the dried plant, not oil-based "catnip-style" products of unknown content.

## If your cat ignores both

Some cats do not respond to any of these plants, and that is fine. Plenty of cats are motivated by other things: food puzzles, a wand toy moved like prey, a sunny window, or simply a new box.
